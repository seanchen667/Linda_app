# References, Originality, and Impact

Reference baseline: November 15, 2026. Product availability and submission rules can change. These sources support background research; inclusion is not endorsement or an instruction to reproduce a clinical device.

## Technical and clinical background

1. [Harvard SEAS: Soft robotic wearable device improves walking for an individual with Parkinson's disease](https://seas.harvard.edu/news/soft-robotic-wearable-device-improves-walking-individual-parkinsons-disease)
   - Describes a waist/thigh-worn, sensor-controlled, cable-driven hip-assistance system.
   - The referenced 2024 study is a single-participant proof of concept, not a guarantee of benefit for others.

2. [Nature Medicine: Soft robotic apparel to avert freezing of gait in Parkinson's disease](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02731-8)
   - Primary research underpinning the above example.
   - Mechanical test-rig results cannot reproduce or establish its clinical findings.

3. [Hocoma Andago: intended use](https://www.hocoma.com/us/solutions/andago/intended-use-indications/)
   - Example of dynamic body-weight support for clinician-supervised gait and balance training.
   - Body-weight support is a separate function from actively assisting hip motion.

4. [Lifeward ReWalk Personal Exoskeleton](https://golifeward.com/products/rewalkpersonal-exoskeleton/)
   - Example of powered hip/knee assistance with population-specific indications, training, and supervision requirements.
   - Not evidence that the same equipment is appropriate for Parkinson's disease or all bedridden individuals.
