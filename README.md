# Linda — Lightweight Hip-Assistance Research

A student-led mechatronics project to design, build, test, and improve a lightweight hip-assistance mechanism.

**Current status:** planning only. No CAD, hardware, firmware, experimental measurements, or clinical validation has been completed in this repository.

## Project question

How can a lightweight, adjustable transmission provide smooth assistance to a simulated leg during forward swing while minimizing resistance when assistance is inactive?

The first deliverable is a **single-joint mechanical test rig**, not a wearable medical device. The rig will use an instrumented simulated leg and secured test masses to compare transmission designs.

## Motivation and scope

The long-term motivation is greater independence for people with mobility limitations, including movement around the home. Walking assistance, body-weight support, balance protection, transfers, and independent toileting are different problems. A hip-assistance module alone does not solve all of them.

Initial work focuses on mechanical performance and control on a test bench. A simulated leg does not reproduce Parkinson's disease, and bench results cannot establish treatment efficacy.

## Development approach

Use short design-build-test cycles inspired by iterative hardware development:

1. Define a measurable question and predict an outcome.
2. Build the simplest testable mechanism.
3. Calibrate instruments and establish a baseline.
4. Test under repeatable conditions and record failures.
5. Change the design for a stated reason.
6. Compare versions, including disadvantages and unexpected results.

Do not treat repeated building as proof of innovation. Original contributions must be identified against existing work and supported by measurements.

## Safety boundary

No human attachment, lifting, body-weight support, gait assistance, or patient testing is authorized by this plan. Initial powered experiments are low-energy, guarded bench tests. Human-use development would require a separate professional engineering review and appropriate clinical, ethics, and regulatory guidance.
